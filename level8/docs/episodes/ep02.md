## Knowledge check

**Q1. What evidence supports a bullish HTF bias?**  
*Answer:* HH/HL sequence, bullish BOS (body close), bullish displacement, reaction from HTF demand/bullish POI, and liquidity objectives above price (e.g., old highs/BSL). No single item is sufficient alone.

**Q2. What is the difference between BOS, CHoCH and MSS in CMI usage?**  
*Answer:* BOS: close beyond the latest swing in the direction of the existing structure (continuation). CHoCH: first close beyond the opposing swing against the existing structure (early warning). MSS: a CHoCH delivered with displacement, typically after a liquidity sweep (stronger evidence, not a guarantee).

**Q3. Does discount automatically mean buy?**  
*Answer:* No. Discount is location within the dealing range. It provides context only; a trade still needs HTF bias alignment, a valid POI, liquidity interaction and LTF confirmation, with defined invalidation.

**Q4. When should a trader stay neutral?**  
*Answer:* When price is range-bound, swings overlap, there is no meaningful displacement, liquidity is resting on both sides, or the market is transitioning between conditions.

## Student exercise

Exercise 1 — Identify HTF Bias. On a Daily FCPO chart: (1) mark the last 3 swing highs and lows; (2) label HH/HL or LH/LL; (3) mark the last BOS (body close); (4) draw the dealing range and equilibrium; (5) state bias as bullish/bearish/neutral with ONE invalidation level. Answer guide: bias must follow the swing sequence; invalidation sits beyond the swing that produced the last BOS; if swings overlap without displacement, the correct answer is neutral.
