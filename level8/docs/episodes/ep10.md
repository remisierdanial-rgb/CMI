## Knowledge check

**Q1. Q1 Why establish HTF bias first?**  
*Answer:* It sets the context in which every LTF signal is interpreted (range, liquidity objectives, invalidation).

**Q2. Q2 HTF vs MTF vs LTF?**  
*Answer:* HTF = direction/context; MTF = setup, structure, location (POI); LTF = confirmation and execution.

**Q3. Q3 Bullish LTF inside bearish HTF?**  
*Answer:* Yes — often a retracement within the HTF leg; it does not by itself reverse the HTF.

**Q4. Q4 IRL vs ERL?**  
*Answer:* ERL beyond the dealing-range boundaries; IRL inside the range (internal swings, internal EQH/EQL, FVGs in ICT usage).

**Q5. Q5 How do BSL/SSL form?**  
*Answer:* Inferred clusters of stop and breakout orders above highs (BSL) and below lows (SSL); not directly observable.

**Q6. Q6 Sweep vs breakout?**  
*Answer:* Sweep: close back inside + opposite displacement + MSS. Breakout: close beyond + retest holds (acceptance) + continuation. Both can fail.

**Q7. Q7 Why is a POI not an entry?**  
*Answer:* It is a location; a setup also needs liquidity interaction, LTF confirmation, execution zone, stop, target.

**Q8. Q8 POI selection factors?**  
*Answer:* The 10-criterion matrix: HTF alignment, structure, P/D, liquidity, displacement, clarity, freshness, distance to target, stop feasibility, R:R.

**Q9. Q9 Why wait for confirmation?**  
*Answer:* It adds evidence at the location and defines structural invalidation; it reduces but never removes risk.

**Q10. Q10 HTF and LTF conflict?**  
*Answer:* Wait, reassess or avoid; do not trade LTF signals against HTF context without a separate tested plan.
