## SCENE 2 · 7.3 · WHAT IS A KILL ZONE (00:22–01:12)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.3 · DEFINITION: “Kill Zones — ICT-defined time windows”.
- Callout “CMI definition”: An ICT Kill Zone is a time window, stated in New York local time, that ICT methodology highlights for monitoring price delivery around major trading sessions. (cue 1).
- List (dot): Time is studied because participation changes through the day (cue 2) · Session opens can bring orders, news and liquidity runs (cue 3) · Not official exchange trading hours (cue 4) · Not a guaranteed opportunity or a signal (cue 5) · Not a universal market law (cue 6).
- Callout “Observe vs interpret”: The clock tells you when to look. Price tells you what is happening. (cue 7).
- **Beats:** (1) An ICT Kill Zone is a time window, stated in New York local time, that the methodology highlights for monitoring price delivery. (2) Time matters because participation changes through the day, as major financial centres open and close. (3) Session opens can bring new orders, scheduled news and runs on liquidity. (4) But a Kill Zone is not an official exchange trading period. (5) It is also not a guaranteed opportunity. (6) And it is not a universal law of markets. (7) Think of it this way: the clock tells you when to look.

## SCENE 5 · 7.3 · EST AND EDT (02:31–03:34)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.3 · TIME ZONES: “New York changes its clock. Malaysia does not.”.
- Table [Zone | UTC offset | MYT difference]: Malaysia Time (MYT) / UTC+8 all year / — (cue 1) ‖ New York Standard (EST) / UTC−5 / MYT = NY + 13 h (cue 2) ‖ New York Daylight (EDT) / UTC−4 / MYT = NY + 12 h (cue 3).
- Table [Year | EDT starts | EDT ends]: 2026 / Sun 8 March / Sun 1 November (cue 4) ‖ 2027 / Sun 14 March / Sun 7 November (cue 4).
- Cards: **15 Jan 2026 · EST** — NY 02:00 → 15:00 MYT; NY 07:00 → 20:00 MYT (cue 5) · **15 Jul 2026 · EDT** — NY 02:00 → 14:00 MYT; NY 07:00 → 19:00 MYT (cue 6).
- Caption: Source: IANA Time Zone Database (America/New_York, Asia/Kuala_Lumpur), verified with Python zoneinfo. US rule: DST from the 2nd Sunday of March to the 1st Sunday of November. (cue 7).
- **Beats:** (1) Malaysia Time is UTC plus eight all year. (2) New York Standard Time, EST, is UTC minus five. (3) New York Daylight Time, EDT, is UTC minus four. (4) In twenty twenty-six, daylight time runs from Sunday the eighth of March to Sunday the first of November. (5) So on the fifteenth of January twenty twenty-six, two a m in New York is three p m in Malaysia. (6) But on the fifteenth of July, two a m in New York is two p m in Malaysia. (7) These dates come from the IANA time zone database.

## SCENE 7 · 7.3 · A SUBTLE TRAP (04:14–04:54)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.3 · DST DETAIL: “The US and UK change clocks on different dates”.
- Table [Period (2026) | New York | London | NY 02:00 equals]: Before 8 Mar / EST (UTC−5) / GMT (UTC+0) / 07:00 London · 15:00 MYT (cue 2) ‖ 8 Mar – 28 Mar / EDT (UTC−4) / GMT (UTC+0) / 06:00 London · 14:00 MYT (cue 3) ‖ 29 Mar – 24 Oct / EDT (UTC−4) / BST (UTC+1) / 07:00 London · 14:00 MYT (cue 4) ‖ 25 Oct – 31 Oct / EDT (UTC−4) / GMT (UTC+0) / 06:00 London · 14:00 MYT (cue 5) ‖ From 1 Nov / EST (UTC−5) / GMT (UTC+0) / 07:00 London · 15:00 MYT (cue 5).
- **Beats:** (1) One more detail that catches many traders. (2) Before the eighth of March, two a m in New York is seven a m in London. (3) From the eighth to the twenty-eighth of March, New York has moved but London has not, so two a m New York is only six a m London. (4) From late March, both are on summer time, and the usual relationship returns. (5) The same mismatch happens for one week in late October.

## SCENE 9 · 7.3 · KILL ZONES AND VOLATILITY (05:39–06:14)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.3 · VOLATILITY: “Why a window can be busy — or quiet”.
- Cards: **Can add activity** — Session opens and overlaps; Higher participation; Scheduled economic releases; Liquidity resting at obvious session highs/lows (cue 1) · **Can reduce activity** — Holidays, thin participation; Waiting for a later news event; Instrument’s main activity is at another time; Price already reached its objective (cue 3).
- Caption: Different instruments have different trading hours and activity profiles. No Kill Zone guarantees volatility. (cue 5).
- **Beats:** (1) Several things can make a window busy: session opens and overlaps, higher participation, scheduled economic releases. (2) And liquidity resting at obvious session highs and lows. (3) Other things make it quiet: holidays, thin participation, or a market waiting for a later announcement. (4) Sometimes an instrument’s main activity simply happens at another time of day. (5) So do not assume every Kill Zone produces high volatility.
