- **On-screen text:**
  - 4.1 · DEFINITIONS FIRST
  - Two definitions from two frameworks
  - WYCKOFF · SPRING — A Phase C event inside an accumulation hypothesis — Price moves below the support of an established trading range — typically defined by an SC, AR and STs — then returns inside the range. It tests remaining supply, and is judged by the Test and the SOS that follow.
  - SMC · LIQUIDITY SWEEP — Price runs through a level where stops may rest — Price trades beyond a swing low (or high), equal lows, or another obvious liquidity level, then moves back. It can occur in a range, a trend, or a pullback. No phase structure is required.

  - 4.3 · TWO DIRECTIONS
  - Why the two terms are not interchangeable
  - SWEEP ⇏ SPRING — Not every sweep is a Spring — Sweeps happen in trends, pullbacks and tiny consolidations. A Spring needs an accumulation hypothesis supported by Phase A and B evidence.
  - SPRING ⇏ "JUST A SWEEP" — Not every Spring should be relabelled — A Spring is judged by Wyckoff criteria — volume, the Test, the SOS. Relabelling it as a sweep can drop that evidence. Check each definition separately.
  - The role of what follows: For both labels, the subsequent price behaviour matters most: a recovery that holds, displacement, and structure change. Without it, neither label is useful.
