## SCENE 2 · 7.6 · WHAT IS SMT (00:22–01:07)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.6 · DEFINITION: “SMT — Smart Money Technique”.
- Callout “CMI definition”: SMT Divergence is a relative comparison of swing highs and lows between related instruments over the same period. A divergence exists when one makes a new extreme and the other does not. (cue 1).
- List (dot): SMT = “Smart Money Technique” in ICT terminology (cue 2) · Related markets usually move together (cue 3) · When they disagree at a swing, that disagreement is information (cue 4) · Not RSI/MACD divergence — no indicator involved (cue 5) · An analytical comparison — not proof of institutional activity (cue 6).
- **Beats:** (1) SMT Divergence is a relative comparison of swing highs and lows between related instruments, over the same period. (2) In ICT terminology, SMT stands for Smart Money Technique. (3) Related markets usually move together. (4) When one makes a new extreme and the other does not, that disagreement is information about relative strength and weakness. (5) This is not RSI or MACD divergence. (6) And it is an analytical comparison.

## SCENE 3 · 7.6 · SELECTING INSTRUMENTS (01:07–01:47)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.6 · INSTRUMENT SELECTION: “Is this pair actually related?”.
- Process flow: 01 Similar underlying exposure (Same or closely linked asset) [cue 1] → 02 Economic relationship (Substitutes, inputs, same index family) [cue 1] → 03 Historical co-movement (Checked, not assumed) [cue 2] → 04 Overlapping trading periods (Both trading at the same time) [cue 3] → 05 Comparable data & specs (Contract, currency, data quality) [cue 4] → 06 Shared fundamental drivers (And known differences) [cue 5].
- Callout “Warning”: Correlation is not constant. Never compare unrelated instruments just because their charts look alike. (cue 6).
- **Beats:** (1) SMT is only as good as the pair. (2) Second, do they have a history of moving together? (3) Third, do they trade at the same time? (4) Fourth, are the data, contract specifications and currencies comparable? (5) Fifth, what drivers do they share, and where do they differ? (6) And remember, correlation is not constant.
