## SCENE 2 · 7.2 · WHAT IS AMD (00:25–01:18)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.2 · DEFINITION: “AMD — a price-behaviour framework”.
- Callout “CMI definition”: AMD describes three phases of price behaviour around a range: accumulation (contained range), manipulation (a false move or liquidity sweep beyond a boundary) and distribution (directional expansion). (cue 1).
- List (dot): Used in ICT / SMC education to read how ranges resolve (cue 2) · Traders study liquidity resting around the range (cue 3) · Not a simple breakout pattern: it expects a false move first (cue 4) · A framework — phases may be messy, partial, or absent (cue 5).
- Table [ | PO3 | AMD]: Anchor / Opening price of a period / The range itself (cue 6) ‖ Question / How is this period delivered? / What is each phase doing? (cue 7).
- **Beats:** (1) AMD describes three phases of price behaviour around a range: a contained range, a false move beyond a boundary, and a directional expansion. (2) It is widely used in ICT and SMC education to read how ranges resolve. (3) Traders study ranges because liquidity tends to rest around their obvious highs and lows. (4) That is the key difference from a simple breakout pattern. (5) And because it is a conceptual framework, the market does not have to complete all three phases neatly, or at all. (6) How does AMD relate to PO3? (7) AMD focuses on the behaviour within each phase, and on the transitions between them.

## SCENE 8 · 7.2 · AMD VS WYCKOFF (05:06–05:59)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.2 · MANDATORY COMPARISON: “AMD (ICT/SMC) vs the Wyckoff Method”.
- Table [ | AMD in ICT / SMC | Wyckoff Method]: Scope / A price-delivery framework: Accumulation, Manipulation, Distribution / Accumulation and Distribution are broader market-cycle structures (phases A–E) (cue 1) ‖ False moves / “Manipulation” = a false move / liquidity-sweep interpretation / Spring and Upthrust are specific, defined Wyckoff events (cue 2) ‖ Focus / Liquidity and directional price delivery / Supply & demand, cause & effect, effort vs result (volume) (cue 3) ‖ Typical use / Intraday and session-based analysis / Can be applied to broader market phases (cue 4) ‖ “Distribution” / The expansion phase (up or down) / A topping structure before markdown (cue 5).
- **Beats:** (1) Now a mandatory comparison. (2) In AMD, manipulation is a general interpretation of a false move. (3) AMD focuses on liquidity and price delivery. (4) AMD is commonly applied intraday and by session. (5) And the word distribution means opposite things.

## SCENE 9 · 7.2 · SIMILAR SHAPE, DIFFERENT FRAMEWORK (05:59–06:32)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.2 · INTERPRETATION: “Similar-looking price, different meaning”.
- Cards: **AMD reading** — Range = accumulation phase; Break below = manipulation (sweep of SSL); Reclaim + displacement = start of distribution; Evidence: liquidity, structure, displacement (cue 1) · **Wyckoff reading** — Range = trading range in a phase schematic; Break below may be a Spring (Phase C); Followed by a test and Sign of Strength; Evidence: volume, effort vs result, phase context (cue 3).
- Caption: Use one framework’s vocabulary at a time — do not mix labels from both on one chart without saying so. (cue 5).
- **Beats:** (1) The same price action can be read by both methods. (2) The evidence comes from liquidity, structure and displacement. (3) A Wyckoff reader may see a trading range, a possible Spring in Phase C, then a test and a sign of strength. (4) Their evidence comes from volume and effort versus result. (5) Both can be useful.
