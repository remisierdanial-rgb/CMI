## SCENE 3 · 7.1 · WHAT IS PO3 (01:02–01:47)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.1 · DEFINITION: “Power of Three — a price-delivery framework”.
- Callout “CMI definition”: Power of Three (PO3) is a conceptual model describing one possible sequence of price delivery within a period: a range near the opening price, a move against the eventual direction, then a directional expansion. (cue 1).
- List (dot): Popularised in modern trading education by ICT (Michael J. Huddleston) (cue 2) · Older ideas — ranges, stop runs, accumulation — are not ICT inventions (e.g. Wyckoff, early 1900s) (cue 3) · Usually drawn as A · M · D: Accumulation, Manipulation, Distribution (cue 4) · A model to interpret price — not a pattern that must appear (cue 5).
- Callout “Model vs observable pattern”: You observe a range, a sweep and an expansion. You interpret them as PO3 phases. (cue 6).
- **Beats:** (1) So what is the Power of Three? (2) The framework was popularised in modern trading education by the Inner Circle Trader, Michael J. (3) However, the underlying ideas, such as trading ranges and runs on stops, are much older. (4) PO3 is usually drawn as three phases: accumulation, manipulation and distribution. (5) It describes one possible sequence. (6) Keep a clear line between what you observe on the chart, and the label you choose to interpret it with.

- Notes panel “Three phases”: Accumulation — price forms a contained range near the open (cue 2) · Liquidity may build above and below obvious highs and lows (cue 3) · Manipulation — price moves beyond one side of the range (cue 4) · Often a sweep of liquidity, against the eventual direction (cue 5) · Distribution — directional expansion away from the range (cue 7) · Structure develops; price may travel toward a liquidity objective (cue 8).
- **Beats:** (1) Let’s build the three phases on a model chart. (2) Phase one is accumulation. (3) Directional progress is limited, and liquidity may build around the obvious range highs and lows. (4) Phase two is manipulation. (5) This may sweep sell-side liquidity resting below the lows, and it moves against the direction that follows. (6) Traders often call this a false breakout or a liquidity raid. (7) Phase three is distribution. (8) Market structure develops, and price may travel toward a liquidity objective, such as an old high.

## SCENE 5 · 7.1 · TWO CLARIFICATIONS (02:39–03:17)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.1 · ACCURACY: “Two words that are easy to misread”.
- Callout ““Manipulation””: A liquidity sweep is something you can see. Calling it “manipulation” is an ICT interpretation.  It does not prove deliberate institutional action, and it says nothing about illegal market manipulation. (cue 1).
- Callout ““Distribution””: In PO3, distribution means the directional expansion phase — up in a bullish model, down in a bearish one.  It is not Wyckoff Distribution, which describes a topping structure. (cue 4).
- **Beats:** (1) Two words in this model need careful handling. (2) A sweep through a level is an observable event. (3) A sweep alone does not prove deliberate institutional action. (4) The second word is distribution. (5) So in a bullish PO3, distribution moves up.

## SCENE 8 · 7.1 · OPENING PRICE (05:38–06:29)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.1 · OPENING PRICE: “Daily open vs session open”.
- Table [Reference | What it is | How CMI uses it]: Daily open / First price of the trading day, by a stated convention (e.g. ICT forex: New York midnight) / Anchor for the day’s PO3: where is price relative to the day’s start? (cue 2) ‖ Session open / First price of a session (e.g. London, New York, or FCPO morning / afternoon) / Anchor for a session-based PO3 inside the day (cue 3) ‖ FCPO note / Day session opens 10:30 MYT; T+1 (after-hours) session 21:00 MYT is booked to the next trading day / State your convention on the chart and keep it consistent (cue 5).
- Callout “Reference — not a wall”: The opening price is not automatic support or resistance, and it is not Equilibrium of a dealing range. (cue 7).
- **Beats:** (1) The opening price is the anchor of the PO3 model, so we need to be precise about which open we mean. (2) The daily open is the first price of the trading day under a stated convention. (3) A session open is the first price of a session, such as London, New York, or an FCPO trading session. (4) A daily PO3 uses the daily open. (5) For FCPO, the day session opens at ten thirty Malaysia time, and the after-hours T plus one session is booked to the next trading day. (6) So choose one convention, write it on your chart, and keep it consistent. (7) And remember: the open is a reference.
