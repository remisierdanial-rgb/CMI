## SCENE 2 · 7.6 · SMT ACROSS TIMEFRAMES (00:19–01:05)

- **Layout:** stack
- Heading — SUBTOPIC 7.6 · MULTIPLE TIMEFRAMES: “Same timeframe, same swings — on both charts”.
- Table [Timeframe | A swing represents | Typical use of SMT]: Daily / Multi-day highs and lows / Context for weekly / daily bias (cue 1) ‖ 4H / Session-to-session swings / Context for the next few sessions (cue 2) ‖ 1H / Intraday swings / Session-level turning points (cue 3) ‖ 15M / Kill Zone-level swings / Lower-timeframe confirmation (cue 4) ‖ Lower (5M, 1M) / Very small swings / Noisy — divergences are frequent (cue 5).
- Callout “Consistency rule”: Compare a 15M swing with a 15M swing, at the same timestamps. A lower-timeframe divergence inside a higher-timeframe trend is a smaller signal, not a reversal of that trend. (cue 6).
- **Beats:** (1) SMT can be read on any timeframe. (2) On four-hour charts, swings are session-to-session. (3) On one-hour charts, swings are intraday turning points. (4) On fifteen-minute charts, swings are Kill Zone-sized, useful as lower-timeframe confirmation. (5) Below that, swings are small and noisy, and divergences are frequent. (6) The rule: compare a fifteen-minute swing with a fifteen-minute swing, at the same timestamps.

- Callout “Why it matters”: If one market is closed, its chart is flat — any “divergence” is an artefact. News released while only one market is open hits that market first. Align timestamps to one time zone before you compare. (cue 4).
- **Beats:** (1) Instruments must be compared during compatible trading periods. (2) Dalian’s day and night sessions use Beijing time, which is the same as Malaysia time.
