## EPISODE 01 — UNDERSTANDING POWER OF THREE

### Knowledge check

**Q1. In one sentence, what does the Power of Three (PO3) framework describe?**  
*Answer:* A possible price-delivery sequence: a range near the open (accumulation), a move against the eventual direction (manipulation), then directional expansion (distribution).  
*Explanation:* PO3 is a conceptual model of how a day or session may unfold. It is a lens for interpretation, not a law that every day must follow.

**Q2. Why is the opening price used as a reference in PO3?**  
*Answer:* It anchors the model: in a bullish PO3 the manipulation tends to trade below the open; in a bearish PO3, above it.  
*Explanation:* The open is a reference point for judging where price is trading relative to the start of the period. It is not automatically support or resistance and it is not Equilibrium.

**Q3. Does a liquidity sweep below a range low prove manipulation by institutions?**  
*Answer:* No. A sweep is an observable price event; "manipulation" is an ICT interpretation, not proof of intent.  
*Explanation:* Stops can be triggered by many participants and by news. CMI treats the label as a model phase name only.

**Q4. How is "distribution" in PO3 different from Wyckoff Distribution?**  
*Answer:* In PO3 it is the directional expansion phase; in Wyckoff it is a topping structure where supply overcomes demand.  
*Explanation:* Same word, different frameworks. Mixing them leads to wrong conclusions (e.g. expecting a top during a bullish PO3 "distribution").

**Q5. Name two signs that a PO3 interpretation should be abandoned.**  
*Answer:* Price keeps going through the swept level with no displacement back; or no structure shift / expansion develops.  
*Explanation:* If the reversal evidence never appears, the sequence is incomplete. Forcing the model is the most common PO3 error.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** On any intraday chart, mark the session open, the first consolidation range, and the range high/low. Label whether price later swept one side.  
*Answer guide:* Correct marking: open as a horizontal reference, range boundaries at the clear highs/lows of the consolidation, and a sweep only if price visibly traded through a boundary.

**Exercise 2.** Find one day where price swept a range boundary and did NOT reverse. Explain why it is not a PO3 example.  
*Answer guide:* No displacement or structure shift followed; price continued in the breakout direction, so the manipulation-distribution sequence never completed.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

Aggregate 15-minute candles of one session into a single candle. Compare its open, high, low and close with the intraday PO3 phases you marked.  
*Answer guide:* The higher-timeframe candle’s open/low/high/close should map onto the intraday open, manipulation extreme, and expansion; if they do not, explain why.

## EPISODE 02 — BULLISH AND BEARISH PO3 MODELS

### Knowledge check

**Q1. List the elements that make a bullish PO3 illustration "complete".**  
*Answer:* Open → range → sweep of sell-side liquidity → reclaim → bullish displacement → BOS/MSS → expansion toward buy-side liquidity.  
*Explanation:* Each element is evidence. Missing reclaim, displacement or structure shift means the sequence is incomplete.

**Q2. Price sweeps the range low and keeps falling. What should you conclude?**  
*Answer:* The bullish PO3 interpretation failed; the sweep was a breakdown, not manipulation.  
*Explanation:* Without a reclaim and displacement there is no evidence of a reversal. Re-assess structure and bias instead of averaging into the idea.

**Q3. Why is an order block or FVG not a mandatory condition for every PO3?**  
*Answer:* They add context and confluence, but the PO3 sequence can occur without them.  
*Explanation:* Making every factor mandatory turns a framework into a rigid checklist and causes missed or forced readings.

**Q4. In the illustrated bullish case, where did accumulation and manipulation sit inside the larger dealing range?**  
*Answer:* In discount — below the equilibrium of the range from the sweep low to the old high.  
*Explanation:* Level 6: buying interest is usually studied in discount. Here it is context that supports, but does not prove, the bullish reading.

**Q5. What is the difference between a failed PO3 and a different structure?**  
*Answer:* A failed PO3 starts the sequence but does not complete it; a different structure never resembled PO3 (e.g. clean breakout trend).  
*Explanation:* Naming the difference stops you from relabelling every chart after the fact.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** Find a bullish day on any instrument. Mark open, range, sweep, reclaim, displacement and MSS in order — write the candle time for each.  
*Answer guide:* All six events should appear in chronological order; if any is missing, label the sequence incomplete.

**Exercise 2.** Find a session where price swept BOTH sides of the opening range. Explain why PO3 is a poor description of that session.  
*Answer guide:* Two-sided sweeps without a sustained expansion show rotation/expansion of range rather than a single manipulation leg.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

On a bearish day, mark the bearish PO3 and add Level 2–6 context: structure, liquidity, order block, FVG and premium/discount. State which factors were present and which were absent.  
*Answer guide:* A strong answer distinguishes confluence present from confluence absent and avoids calling absent factors “hidden”.

## EPISODE 03 — ACCUMULATION, MANIPULATION, DISTRIBUTION

### Knowledge check

**Q1. How does the focus of AMD differ from the focus of PO3?**  
*Answer:* PO3 anchors the sequence to the opening price of a period; AMD studies the behaviour inside each phase and the transitions between them.  
*Explanation:* They describe the same three-phase idea from different angles. Keeping the focus separate avoids treating them as two different signals for the same thing.

**Q2. Give three observable characteristics of an accumulation phase.**  
*Answer:* Sideways price, repeated highs/lows inside clear boundaries, reduced directional progress (also: possible EQH/EQL).  
*Explanation:* These are observable facts. Whether participants were “accumulating” is an interpretation.

**Q3. Is every consolidation range institutional accumulation?**  
*Answer:* No. Ranges form for many reasons (low participation, waiting for news, balance of orders).  
*Explanation:* CMI treats “accumulation” as a phase label in a model, not a claim about who was buying.

**Q4. What is the difference between a Wyckoff Spring and an AMD manipulation?**  
*Answer:* A Spring is a specific event inside Wyckoff’s phased accumulation schematic; AMD manipulation is a general ICT label for a false move/liquidity sweep before expansion.  
*Explanation:* They can look similar on a chart, but each sits in a different framework with different supporting evidence (e.g. Wyckoff volume/effort analysis).

**Q5. After a sweep and reclaim, what evidence would you want before calling the distribution phase?**  
*Answer:* Displacement, a structure shift (MSS/BOS) and price moving away from the range toward external liquidity.  
*Explanation:* Without expansion, a reclaim can simply return price to the middle of the range.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** Mark a consolidation range and label EQH or EQL only where two or more highs/lows are genuinely equal (within one or two ticks).  
*Answer guide:* EQH/EQL should be drawn only where the extremes match closely; approximate “double tops” far apart are not equal highs.

**Exercise 2.** Find a range that broke out and never returned. Explain why it is not an AMD manipulation.  
*Answer guide:* No reclaim into the range and no reversal displacement: the breakout was continuation.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

Draw an AMD vs Wyckoff comparison for one range you have marked: list what each framework would label, and what evidence each would need.  
*Answer guide:* AMD: range, sweep, reclaim, displacement, expansion. Wyckoff: phases A–E, Spring/Upthrust, volume and effort-vs-result. Note where the labels disagree.

## EPISODE 04 — UNDERSTANDING ICT KILL ZONES

### Knowledge check

**Q1. What is an ICT Kill Zone, and what is it not?**  
*Answer:* A time window (New York local time) that ICT methodology highlights for monitoring price; it is not an exchange trading session and not a guarantee of movement or a setup.  
*Explanation:* Kill Zones are an analytical convention. Exchanges define trading hours; ICT educators define Kill Zones, and their definitions vary.

**Q2. Convert the London Kill Zone (02:00–05:00 New York) to Malaysia time on 15 January 2026 and on 15 July 2026.**  
*Answer:* 15 Jan 2026 (EST, +13): 15:00–18:00 MYT. 15 Jul 2026 (EDT, +12): 14:00–17:00 MYT.  
*Explanation:* Malaysia (UTC+8) has no DST; New York moves between UTC−5 (EST) and UTC−4 (EDT). In 2026 EDT runs 8 March to 1 November.

**Q3. Why can one fixed Malaysia-time schedule for New York Kill Zones be wrong for part of the year?**  
*Answer:* Because New York changes its clock for daylight saving; the MYT equivalent shifts by one hour twice a year.  
*Explanation:* A schedule built in July (EDT) will be one hour early in January (EST) if not updated.

**Q4. FCPO’s afternoon session (14:30–18:00 MYT) overlaps the London Kill Zone in Malaysia time. Does that make it a “London Kill Zone” for FCPO?**  
*Answer:* No. Clock overlap is not the same as the session definition; FCPO has its own exchange sessions and drivers.  
*Explanation:* ICT Kill Zones were defined around FX/US index markets. For FCPO, time-of-day behaviour must be studied and tested on FCPO data itself.

**Q5. Name two reasons a Kill Zone can be quiet.**  
*Answer:* No scheduled news / holiday conditions; the instrument’s main participation happens at another time; price is waiting for a later event.  
*Explanation:* Time windows describe when activity is often studied — not when it must occur.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** On a 30-minute chart in New York time, shade the Asian, London and NY AM Kill Zones for one day, and mark the Asian high and low.  
*Answer guide:* Shading should match the CMI schedule (20:00–00:00, 02:00–05:00, 07:00–10:00 NY); Asian high/low are the extremes inside the Asian window only.

**Exercise 2.** Mark FCPO’s morning, afternoon and T+1 sessions on an FCPO chart in Malaysia time, and write the New York equivalent for today’s date.  
*Answer guide:* Use the official Bursa hours (10:30–12:30, 14:30–18:00, T+1 21:00–23:30 MYT) and subtract 12 h (EDT) or 13 h (EST) depending on the date.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

Build your own Kill Zone conversion card for the current month: NY window → MYT, including the date New York next changes its clock.  
*Answer guide:* Check the date against an IANA-based source. In 2026 the change dates are 8 March and 1 November; in 2027, 14 March and 7 November.

## EPISODE 05 — KILL ZONES AND MARKET SESSION APPLICATION

### Knowledge check

**Q1. What are the Asian high and Asian low, and how are they used?**  
*Answer:* The highest high and lowest low printed inside the Asian window (20:00–00:00 NY); later sessions may use them as liquidity references.  
*Explanation:* They are reference levels for later sessions, not targets that must be hit.

**Q2. Describe a bullish London scenario using the Asian range.**  
*Answer:* London trades below the Asian low (sweep), reverses with displacement and expands higher, often setting the day’s low early.  
*Explanation:* This is one possible behaviour; London may also continue through the Asian low.

**Q3. Why does the NY AM window often show news-related volatility?**  
*Answer:* Many scheduled US economic releases are at 08:30 New York time, inside the NY AM window.  
*Explanation:* News can cause sweeps in both directions; a large candle at release time is not by itself a model signal.

**Q4. What is session range mapping?**  
*Answer:* Marking Asian, London and NY highs/lows plus PDH/PDL so that each session’s extremes become reference levels.  
*Explanation:* Mapping turns time into price references, which keeps the analysis anchored to liquidity rather than the clock.

**Q5. London trends down all session and NY continues lower. Which “model” failed, and what is the correct response?**  
*Answer:* The expected London sweep-and-reverse model failed; follow what the chart shows (continuation) and do not force a reversal.  
*Explanation:* Session models are possibilities. Observing actual structure is the priority.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** For one week, record the Asian high/low and note whether London traded through either side. Tally the outcomes.  
*Answer guide:* A fair tally includes “neither” and “both” outcomes; this shows how often the textbook sweep actually occurs.

**Exercise 2.** Map Asian, London and NY ranges plus PDH/PDL on one day. Identify which level was reached last.  
*Answer guide:* All five reference levels should be drawn at exact session extremes in New York time.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

Pick a day with a high-impact US release at 08:30 NY. Describe the behaviour before, during and after the release, and whether any Kill Zone model was valid.  
*Answer guide:* Note abnormal wicks, both-side sweeps and widened spreads; a strong answer explains why the model might be suspended around news.

## EPISODE 06 — UNDERSTANDING DAILY BIAS

### Knowledge check

**Q1. Define Daily Bias in one sentence.**  
*Answer:* A working hypothesis about the likely direction of the day, built from higher-timeframe structure, liquidity objectives and price location.  
*Explanation:* It is updated as evidence changes; it is not a forecast and not an entry signal.

**Q2. What is a Draw on Liquidity (DOL)?**  
*Answer:* The liquidity pool price is judged most likely to move toward next (e.g. PDH, PWH, EQH, an old high or low).  
*Explanation:* It is an analytical target used to frame the day, never a guaranteed destination.

**Q3. Price is bullish on the daily chart but trading in premium at a bearish PD array. How does that affect a bullish bias?**  
*Answer:* It weakens it: location is unfavourable for new buying, so the bias may be held with lower conviction or set to neutral until price reaches discount.  
*Explanation:* Level 6 location (premium/discount) is one of the inputs; conflicting inputs reduce confidence.

**Q4. Give three situations that justify a neutral Daily Bias.**  
*Answer:* Conflicting HTF structure; price at equilibrium between two liquidity pools; no displacement; major event risk; overlapping conflicting PD arrays.  
*Explanation:* Neutral is a valid conclusion. Forcing a direction is a common and costly error.

**Q5. What is an invalidation condition, and why must it be written before the session?**  
*Answer:* The price behaviour that would prove the bias wrong (e.g. a daily close below the HL that defines bullish structure). Writing it first prevents rationalising.  
*Explanation:* A bias without invalidation cannot be tested and tends to be changed after every candle.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** On a daily chart, label the last three swing highs and lows (HH/HL or LH/LL) and the latest BOS. State the structural bias.  
*Answer guide:* Labels must sit on true swing extremes; the latest BOS defines whether structure is bullish or bearish.

**Exercise 2.** Draw the dealing range from the latest significant swing low to swing high. Mark EQ, premium, discount and OTE (62–79%). Where is price now?  
*Answer guide:* EQ is the 50% level; OTE spans 62–79% retracement; state the current location in words.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

Complete the 10-question CMI Daily Bias checklist for tomorrow on one market, including an explicit invalidation level.  
*Answer guide:* A good checklist ends with bullish/bearish/neutral and a clear price-based invalidation; it lists the event calendar.

## EPISODE 07 — DAILY BIAS AND DRAW ON LIQUIDITY

### Knowledge check

**Q1. List six liquidity references you would map before a session.**  
*Answer:* PDH, PDL, PWH, PWL, EQH/EQL, previous swing highs/lows (plus internal FVGs).  
*Explanation:* Mapping first prevents choosing a target after the fact.

**Q2. How do you prioritise between several possible liquidity objectives?**  
*Answer:* Alignment with HTF structure/bias, HTF significance (weekly > daily > intraday), proximity/realistic reach, and how clean/untouched the level is.  
*Explanation:* A distant weekly level may be the larger draw, but the nearer daily level may be the realistic objective for today.

**Q3. Why might price visit internal liquidity before reaching an external objective?**  
*Answer:* Price often rebalances imbalances (FVGs) or retraces into discount/premium before continuing.  
*Explanation:* Objectives are rarely reached in a straight line; expecting that leads to premature exits or invalidation.

**Q4. How can PO3 be used inside a Daily Bias?**  
*Answer:* As one possible interpretation of how the day is delivered: open → accumulation → manipulation against the bias → distribution toward the daily objective.  
*Explanation:* PO3 is a lens on delivery; the bias still comes from HTF context.

**Q5. Can a Kill Zone set the Daily Bias by itself? Explain.**  
*Answer:* No. A Kill Zone is a time to observe; bias comes from structure, liquidity and location.  
*Explanation:* Time is context for when evidence may appear, not evidence of direction.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** On a daily chart, map PDH, PDL, PWH, PWL and any genuine EQH/EQL. Rank the top two objectives for tomorrow and justify.  
*Answer guide:* Ranking should cite bias alignment, significance, distance and cleanliness.

**Exercise 2.** Find an example where price reached a liquidity objective and then changed behaviour (consolidated or reversed). Mark where the change began.  
*Answer guide:* The change should appear after the objective was traded through, e.g. a failure to continue followed by displacement the other way.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

Write a one-paragraph Daily Bias for tomorrow that includes: structure, DOL, location, a PO3 expectation for the session, which Kill Zone you will watch, and invalidation.  
*Answer guide:* Each element should be explicit; the Kill Zone must be described as a time to observe, not a reason for direction.

## EPISODE 08 — UNDERSTANDING JUDAS SWING

### Knowledge check

**Q1. Define a Judas Swing in ICT terms.**  
*Answer:* An initial, counter-directional move early in a session (often from the New York midnight or session open) that runs liquidity before price moves in the direction of the Daily Bias.  
*Explanation:* It is an interpretation that requires bias, timing and subsequent behaviour — not a pattern that can be confirmed at the moment of the sweep.

**Q2. Is every liquidity sweep a Judas Swing? Explain.**  
*Answer:* No. A sweep is any trade through a relevant high/low; a Judas Swing additionally needs session timing, a Daily Bias opposite to the initial move, and subsequent displacement/structure in the bias direction.  
*Explanation:* A Judas Swing may involve a sweep, but most sweeps do not meet the other conditions.

**Q3. On a bullish day, what does the bullish Judas Swing look like relative to the midnight open?**  
*Answer:* Price first trades below the midnight open (the false move down), sweeps sell-side liquidity, then reverses with displacement above.  
*Explanation:* The midnight/session open is the reference; trading below it on a bullish day creates the “discount” for the expected rally.

**Q4. Why is the initial sweep not an entry signal by itself?**  
*Answer:* At the moment of the sweep there is no evidence of reversal; displacement and an MSS/BOS are still required.  
*Explanation:* Failed Judas Swings — sweeps that continue — are common.

**Q5. Convert the London window (02:00–05:00 NY) where a Judas Swing is often studied, to MYT in February and in August 2026.**  
*Answer:* February (EST): 15:00–18:00 MYT. August (EDT): 14:00–17:00 MYT.  
*Explanation:* 2026 EDT runs 8 March – 1 November; MYT = NY + 13 h in EST, + 12 h in EDT.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** On a 30-minute chart in NY time, mark the midnight open, the Asian range and the first London move. Was the first move with or against your Daily Bias?  
*Answer guide:* Correct marking uses 00:00 NY as the open; the first move is “against bias” only if bias was set before the session.

**Exercise 2.** Find a day where the initial session move swept liquidity and continued. Label why it was not a Judas Swing.  
*Answer guide:* No reversal displacement / no MSS; the sweep continued in the same direction, so the interpretation failed.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

For five sessions, record: bias (pre-set), first move direction vs bias, liquidity swept, displacement yes/no, MSS yes/no, and outcome.  
*Answer guide:* The log should show how many “first moves against bias” actually became Judas Swings; expect many did not.

## EPISODE 09 — BULLISH AND BEARISH JUDAS SWING MODELS

### Knowledge check

**Q1. List the ten annotations of a complete bullish Judas Swing chart.**  
*Answer:* HTF bullish structure, Daily Bias, relevant liquidity, session timing, initial down move, SSL sweep, bullish displacement, MSS/BOS, FVG (if present), move toward BSL.  
*Explanation:* Each annotation is a piece of evidence; missing pieces lower confidence or invalidate the reading.

**Q2. In the bearish model, which liquidity is swept first and which is the objective?**  
*Answer:* Buy-side liquidity is swept first (above the Asian high/session high); sell-side liquidity below is the objective.  
*Explanation:* The initial move runs against the bearish bias and takes buy stops before the expected decline.

**Q3. A sweep of the Asian low happens at 12:30 New York time. Why might you not treat it as a Judas Swing?**  
*Answer:* It is outside the session-open windows where the model is defined (it is in NY lunch), so the timing context is missing.  
*Explanation:* Judas Swing is a session-opening concept; a sweep at an arbitrary time is just a sweep.

**Q4. What is the correct decision when the Daily Bias is unclear and a sweep occurs?**  
*Answer:* No Judas Swing interpretation: “counter-bias” cannot be defined without a bias. Stand aside or treat it as a plain sweep.  
*Explanation:* The concept depends on a pre-existing directional hypothesis.

**Q5. Why is an FVG described as “where present” in the model?**  
*Answer:* Displacement does not always leave a clean FVG; its absence does not by itself invalidate the reading.  
*Explanation:* Treating optional confluence as mandatory leads to forcing or missing valid examples.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** Annotate one bullish and one bearish Judas Swing example with all ten labels, using NY time on the x-axis.  
*Answer guide:* Labels must be in chronological order and each must correspond to visible candles.

**Exercise 2.** Find a “no-trade” example: a session-open sweep without displacement. Explain your decision.  
*Answer guide:* Without displacement and MSS there is no evidence of reversal; the correct decision is no trade.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

Write a decision table for your own trading plan: for each of the five no-trade scenarios, define what you will do and what evidence would change your mind.  
*Answer guide:* Good tables state observable conditions (e.g. “no close above the last lower high within the Kill Zone”).

## EPISODE 10 — UNDERSTANDING SMT DIVERGENCE

### Knowledge check

**Q1. What does SMT stand for in ICT methodology, and what does SMT Divergence compare?**  
*Answer:* Smart Money Technique; it compares the swing highs/lows of two (or more) related instruments over the same period.  
*Explanation:* It is a relative price comparison between instruments — not a price-vs-indicator comparison.

**Q2. State CMI’s bullish SMT convention.**  
*Answer:* Instrument A trades below its prior swing low (lower low) while related Instrument B fails to make a corresponding lower low (holds a higher low). Confirmation is sought on A.  
*Explanation:* Conventions vary on which instrument “leads”; stating the convention prevents confusion.

**Q3. Give four criteria for choosing a valid SMT pair.**  
*Answer:* A real economic relationship, historical co-movement, overlapping trading hours, comparable data quality (also: contract specs, currency, drivers).  
*Explanation:* Charts that merely look similar are not a valid basis for SMT.

**Q4. How is SMT different from RSI or MACD divergence?**  
*Answer:* SMT compares two instruments’ prices; oscillator divergence compares one instrument’s price with an indicator derived from that price.  
*Explanation:* They are separate techniques measuring different things (relative strength vs momentum).

**Q5. Why is SMT not an automatic reversal signal?**  
*Answer:* Divergences can persist or resolve without reversal; structure confirmation (e.g. MSS) and context are still required.  
*Explanation:* Correlations change, news can hit one instrument more, and both may continue in the same direction.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** Place two related instruments side by side on the same timeframe and time range. Mark the last two swing lows on both and state whether bullish SMT exists.  
*Answer guide:* Bullish SMT requires A: lower low and B: higher (or equal-holding) low at the corresponding time.

**Exercise 2.** Repeat for swing highs and identify bearish SMT. Note the exact candle times of each swing on both charts.  
*Answer guide:* Swings must be time-aligned; a mismatch in time invalidates the comparison.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

Choose a pair you believe is related. Write a one-page justification covering the economic link, trading hours, contract/currency differences and a simple co-movement check.  
*Answer guide:* A strong justification acknowledges when the relationship is weak or unstable.

## EPISODE 11 — BULLISH AND BEARISH SMT DIVERGENCE

### Knowledge check

**Q1. In the bullish case study, what exactly is the “discrepancy” that defines SMT?**  
*Answer:* At the same swing (same time), Instrument A trades below its prior low while Instrument B stays above its prior low.  
*Explanation:* The comparison is swing-to-swing at the same time on both charts.

**Q2. Both instruments make lower lows at swing 2. Is there SMT? What does it suggest?**  
*Answer:* No SMT; both confirm the bearish move, suggesting continuation is at least as likely as reversal.  
*Explanation:* Agreement between related instruments is the normal state; only disagreement is SMT.

**Q3. Why are SMT readings less reliable between weakly correlated instruments?**  
*Answer:* Their swings often diverge for unrelated reasons, so a divergence carries little information.  
*Explanation:* SMT assumes the instruments would normally confirm each other.

**Q4. Give an example of a news-driven divergence.**  
*Answer:* A data release affecting one market more than the other (e.g. country-specific data or a commodity-specific report) makes one instrument move alone.  
*Explanation:* Such divergences reflect different fundamental shocks rather than hidden strength or weakness.

**Q5. An SMT forms but both instruments then continue lower. What went wrong in the analysis, if anything?**  
*Answer:* Possibly nothing: SMT is a warning, not a signal. Without structure confirmation, the correct stance was to wait.  
*Explanation:* Divergence that does not lead to reversal is normal; the error would be acting without confirmation.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** Recreate the eight-step bullish case on a real pair of your choice. Record the timestamps of swing 1 and swing 2 on both charts.  
*Answer guide:* Timestamps must match; otherwise the comparison is not synchronised.

**Exercise 2.** Find a divergence on your pair that did not lead to a reversal. Describe the subsequent structure.  
*Answer guide:* Typically no MSS forms on A, and both instruments resume the original direction.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

Over twenty sessions, log every SMT you see on one pair: type, timeframe, PD-array context, confirmation (yes/no), and outcome.  
*Answer guide:* The log should include divergences without confirmation and without reversal — they are part of the evidence.

## EPISODE 12 — ADVANCED SMT APPLICATION AND LIMITATIONS

### Knowledge check

**Q1. Why must the timeframe and swing selection be identical on both instruments?**  
*Answer:* SMT compares the same swing at the same time; mixing timeframes compares different swings and creates false divergences.  
*Explanation:* A 15M swing low on one chart is not comparable with a 1H swing low on the other.

**Q2. FCPO’s T+1 session is 21:00–23:30 MYT. Which comparative markets overlap with it?**  
*Answer:* DCE night session (21:00–23:00 Beijing = MYT) and part of CBOT soybean oil trading (CBOT day session opens 08:30 CT = 21:30 MYT during CDT, 22:30 MYT during CST).  
*Explanation:* Only overlapping periods can be compared candle-for-candle.

**Q3. List five differences between FCPO and CBOT soybean oil that complicate SMT.**  
*Answer:* Different underlying oil, currency (MYR vs USD cents/lb), contract size (25 t vs 60,000 lb), trading hours/time zones, and fundamental drivers (palm vs soy supply, biodiesel policy).  
*Explanation:* Each difference can cause divergence that has nothing to do with “smart money”.

**Q4. If no reliable historical example is available, how should an FCPO SMT case be presented?**  
*Answer:* As a clearly labelled illustrative scenario, with no claim of historical accuracy or of a stable correlation.  
*Explanation:* Fabricated data or implied correlations would mislead students.

**Q5. Is there a universal numerical threshold for a valid SMT (e.g. correlation above 0.8)?**  
*Answer:* No. CMI does not set one without credible testing; validity is judged by relationship, alignment, swing selection, context and confirmation.  
*Explanation:* Any threshold would need to be tested on the specific pair and timeframe.

### Chart-identification exercises

**Exercise 1.** Put FCPO and DCE palm olein (or your chosen comparator) on the same timeframe in MYT. Shade only the periods when both are trading.  
*Answer guide:* Correct shading shows overlaps only (e.g. 10:30–11:30, 14:30–15:00, 21:00–23:00 MYT for the DCE day/night sessions — verify current hours).

**Exercise 2.** Within an overlap period, mark corresponding swing highs/lows on both and state whether any divergence exists.  
*Answer guide:* Swings must be marked at the same timestamps; if none diverge, record “no SMT”.

### Practical market interpretation exercise

Complete the eight-step FCPO SMT pre-use checklist for one comparator, including a simple co-movement check and alternative explanations.  
*Answer guide:* A strong answer cites the contract specs from official sources and states clearly where the relationship is weak.

## EPISODE 13 — INTEGRATION OF ALL LEVEL 7 CONCEPTS

### Knowledge check

**Q1. Put these in CMI workflow order: Kill Zone context, Daily Bias, Displacement, HTF structure, PD arrays, Invalidation.**  
*Answer:* HTF structure → Daily Bias → PD arrays → Kill Zone context → Displacement → Invalidation (with DOL, dealing range, premium/discount, PO3/AMD, Judas, SMT in between).  
*Explanation:* Context is built from the top down; timing and confirmation come after the directional hypothesis.

**Q2. Does every analysis need all twelve workflow elements?**  
*Answer:* No. The workflow is a structured process, not a checklist that must be fully ticked.  
*Explanation:* Forcing every element produces imaginary confluence.

**Q3. In the integrated bullish example, why was SMT not used?**  
*Answer:* No verified related instrument was available for that illustration; CMI does not invent one.  
*Explanation:* SMT is optional and only valid with a genuine, synchronised comparator.

**Q4. Daily Bias is bullish but the 15M structure is bearish and no displacement appears in the Kill Zone. What is the CMI conclusion?**  
*Answer:* Neutral / no trade until the conflict resolves.  
*Explanation:* Conflicting evidence lowers confidence; neutrality is a valid outcome.

**Q5. Where does PO3/AMD fit in the integrated workflow?**  
*Answer:* As an interpretation of how the session is being delivered (step 8), after context and before Judas/SMT/confirmation.  
*Explanation:* It describes delivery; it does not set direction by itself.

## Level 7 — Comprehensive Assessment

Students should be able to demonstrate each competency below. Suggested evidence and model answers are given.

**1. Explain Power of Three.**  
*Model answer:* A conceptual price-delivery model: range near the open, counter-move, directional expansion. Popularised by ICT; not a law.

**2. Identify Accumulation, Manipulation and Distribution.**  
*Model answer:* Range with clear boundaries; sweep beyond one boundary and return; displacement and expansion with structure.

**3. Distinguish PO3 from AMD and from Wyckoff.**  
*Model answer:* PO3 anchors to the open of a period; AMD studies the phases of a range; Wyckoff uses phases A–E, Spring/Upthrust and volume (effort vs result). “Distribution” means expansion in PO3/AMD but a top in Wyckoff.

**4. Identify bullish and bearish PO3 models.**  
*Model answer:* Bullish: below the open first, SSL sweep, expansion up to BSL. Bearish: the inverse.

**5. Explain ICT Kill Zones.**  
*Model answer:* ICT-defined NY-time windows for monitoring price (CMI: Asian 20–24, London 02–05, NY AM 07–10, Lunch 12–13, NY PM 13:30–16); not exchange hours, not guarantees.

**6. Convert New York time to Malaysia time correctly.**  
*Model answer:* MYT = NY + 13 h during EST, + 12 h during EDT (e.g. NY 02:00 = 15:00 MYT on 15 Jan 2026; 14:00 MYT on 15 Jul 2026).

**7. Explain the impact of daylight saving time.**  
*Model answer:* US DST runs 2nd Sunday of March to 1st Sunday of November (2026: 8 Mar–1 Nov); Malaysia has no DST, so MYT equivalents shift by 1 h; US/UK change on different dates.

**8. Distinguish ICT Kill Zones from official exchange sessions.**  
*Model answer:* FCPO: 10:30–12:30, 14:30–18:00, T+1 21:00–23:30 MYT per Bursa; clock overlap with a Kill Zone is not a definition.

**9. Develop bullish, bearish and neutral Daily Bias.**  
*Model answer:* Use the 10-question checklist; neutral when structure conflicts, price is at EQ, no displacement or event risk.

**10. Identify the Draw on Liquidity.**  
*Model answer:* Choose among PDH/PDL, PWH/PWL, EQH/EQL, swings by bias alignment, significance, reach and cleanliness.

**11. Map previous day and previous week highs and lows.**  
*Model answer:* Extremes of the prior daily/weekly candle under a stated session convention.

**12. Explain the Judas Swing.**  
*Model answer:* Initial counter-bias move early in a session from a defined open, often a sweep, confirmed later by displacement and structure.

**13. Distinguish Judas Swing from an ordinary liquidity sweep.**  
*Model answer:* A sweep is any trade through a level; Judas additionally requires bias, timing and confirmation.

**14. Identify bullish and bearish SMT Divergence.**  
*Model answer:* Bullish: A lower low, B higher low; bearish: A higher high, B lower high — at time-aligned swings.

**15. Select appropriate related instruments for SMT.**  
*Model answer:* Real economic link, co-movement, overlapping hours, comparable data/specs, known driver differences.

**16. Explain why SMT requires synchronised data.**  
*Model answer:* Comparisons must be at the same timestamps and timeframe; closed or thin markets create artefacts.

**17. Integrate Level 7 with previous CMI levels.**  
*Model answer:* Structure (L2), liquidity (L3), institutional zones (L4), displacement/FVG (L5), premium/discount & PD arrays (L6) as context.

**18. Apply the concepts to FCPO analysis.**  
*Model answer:* Use Bursa sessions and specs (25 t, RM1, RM25/tick); treat Judas-style readings and SMT with DCE/CBOT as tested hypotheses only.

**19. Identify failed or incomplete models.**  
*Model answer:* No reclaim, no displacement, no structure shift, two-sided sweeps, continuation through the swept level.

**20. Explain the limitations of ICT concepts.**  
*Model answer:* They are interpretive frameworks; labels such as manipulation do not prove intent; they must be validated on the specific market and tested.
