S01.1 | Welcome to Video 4. We start the second subtopic: the Intraday SOP.
S01.2 | Intraday trading needs more thorough daily planning than scalping.
S01.3 | We will define intraday trading, build the timeframe framework, and a nine-step preparation list.
S01.4 | Then we build the daily bias, the dealing range, Premium and Discount, and primary and alternative scenarios.
S01.5 | Finally, we separate a potential setup from an actionable entry.
S02.1 | What is intraday trading? Positions are opened and closed within the same trading day.
S02.2 | Compared with scalping, intraday trading follows 1H and 30M structure, so structural stop-losses are usually wider and targets further away.
S02.3 | Intraday trading depends on session behaviour: session highs and lows, PDH and PDL, and the daily range.
S02.4 | Daily preparation is essential, because the plan is made before the session, not while price is moving.
S02.5 | The trader must decide whether every position will be closed before the end of the day or before the end of the session.
S02.6 | Remember, the holding period is set by the trading plan, not by the chart timeframe. An intraday position held overnight has become a swing trade, with different risks.
S03.1 | The CMI intraday timeframe framework for FCPO.
S03.2 | The Daily and 4H give directional context: the larger structure, major liquidity and the dealing range.
S03.3 | The 1H and 30M charts are used for market structure and the setup.
S03.4 | The 15M chart is used for location, meaning more precise liquidity and POIs, and the price reaction.
S03.5 | The 5M chart is used for entry confirmation.
S03.6 | Once again, this is a suggested framework, not a mandatory rule.
S04.1 | Intraday preparation, in nine steps.
S04.2 | One, review the Daily and 4H charts. Two, identify the broader market structure.
S04.3 | Three, mark the major liquidity. Four, mark PDH and PDL.
S04.4 | Five, mark the relevant POIs. Six, build bullish, bearish or neutral scenarios.
S04.5 | Seven, identify today's important market events. Eight, define the maximum risk for the day.
S04.6 | Nine, prepare one primary scenario and one alternative scenario.
S05.1 | Here is an example of two scenarios written before the session.
S05.2 | Scenario A, the primary scenario: the Daily is bullish. If price sweeps Sell-Side Liquidity below PDL and shows confirmation, we look for buying opportunities towards PDH.
S05.3 | Scenario B, the alternative: if the 1H closes below the major higher low, the bullish bias is cancelled. No buying opportunities; we wait or reassess.
S05.4 | With two scenarios, decisions during the session become a choice between plans already written, not spontaneous reactions.
S06.1 | Next, the intraday directional bias.
S06.2 | A bullish bias: Daily and 4H structure is rising, and price is in discount or near a bullish POI.
S06.3 | A bearish bias is the opposite. A neutral bias means the context is unclear, so trade size or frequency is reduced, or there are no trades at all.
S06.4 | Alignment between the higher timeframe and intraday adds clarity, but it does not guarantee the outcome.
S06.5 | Draw on Liquidity is the liquidity objective expected to attract price, for example PDH. It is a hypothesis, not a certainty.
S06.6 | Every bias must have an invalidation condition, for example a 1H close below the major higher low.
S07.1 | The daily dealing range is drawn from a selected swing low to a selected swing high.
S07.2 | The fifty percent line is equilibrium. The area above it is premium, and the area below it is discount.
S07.3 | With a bullish bias, we are more interested in buying in discount, near a POI and Sell-Side Liquidity.
S07.4 | The Draw on Liquidity in this example is the Buy-Side Liquidity above the range high, a potential opposing target.
S08.1 | When should an intraday bias change?
S08.2 | On the left, several 5M candles move against the bias, but the 1H structure holds. This is noise, and the bias does not need to change.
S08.3 | On the right, the 1H closes below the major higher low, the invalidation condition that was written down. This is meaningful new information.
S08.4 | The bias can change when meaningful price information appears, but not because of every small move on the lower timeframe.
S09.1 | An intraday setup combines several components.
S09.2 | Higher-timeframe context, then market structure on the 1H or 30M.
S09.3 | Liquidity, and the selected POI.
S09.4 | Premium or Discount, displacement and the FVG.
S09.5 | Together these form a hypothesis. It is not yet an entry.
S10.1 | What is the difference between a potential setup and an actionable entry?
S10.2 | Both need the right context and the right location.
S10.3 | But an actionable entry also needs a liquidity interaction, an MSS and displacement on the chosen timeframe.
S10.4 | It also needs a clear execution zone, a clear invalidation, and a calculated risk-to-reward.
S10.5 | If any condition is not yet met, the setup remains an observation, not a trade.
S11.2 | Question one: why should each trading style have its own SOP?
S11.3 | Answer: invalidation, targets, relative costs, monitoring and position size are different for each style.
S11.4 | Question two: what determines the intraday holding period?
S11.5 | Answer: the trading plan, meaning the close-out rule, not the chart timeframe.
S11.6 | Question three: when should an intraday bias change?
S11.7 | Answer: when the written invalidation condition is met, or meaningful new information appears, not on every 5M move.
S12.2 | Intraday means same-day exposure, with a close-out rule written into the plan.
S12.3 | Daily and 4H context, 1H and 30M setup, 15M location, and 5M entry.
S12.4 | Bias, dealing range, Draw on Liquidity and invalidation, together with primary and alternative scenarios.
S12.5 | A potential setup is not yet an actionable entry.
