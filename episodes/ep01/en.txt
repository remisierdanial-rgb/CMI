S01.1 | Welcome to Level 9: SMC Trading Strategy.
S01.2 | In Levels 1 to 8, we built the foundations: market structure, liquidity, POIs, Fair Value Gaps, and Premium and Discount.
S01.3 | Level 9 answers one important question: how do we organise all of these concepts into a trading strategy with clear rules?
S01.4 | In this video, we will look at four things.
S01.5 | First, the difference between a concept, a setup and a trading plan.
S01.6 | Second, three trading styles, each of which needs its own SOP.
S01.7 | Third, the CMI trading workflow, from market context all the way to the journal.
S01.8 | And fourth, an FCPO example built step by step, without looking at future candles.
S02.1 | Many students can identify an Order Block or an FVG on a chart. But identifying a setup is not yet a strategy.
S02.2 | A concept is an analytical tool. For example, a liquidity sweep, or a market structure shift.
S02.3 | A setup is a combination of several concepts at one specific location, forming a trading hypothesis.
S02.4 | A trading plan sets the rules: when to enter, where the stop-loss goes, what the target is, how much to risk, and when not to trade at all.
S02.5 | An SOP, or Standard Operating Procedure, is a trading plan written as ordered steps, so it can be repeated, recorded and evaluated.
S02.6 | The goal of Level 9 is not just to find setups, but to build an SOP that can be tested.
S03.1 | Level 9 contains six subtopics, and their original order is preserved.
S03.2 | One, Scalping SOP. Two, Intraday SOP. Three, Swing Trading SOP.
S03.3 | The first three subtopics are separated because each style has different timeframes, holding periods, stop-loss sizes and position management.
S03.4 | Four, Entry Confirmation. Five, Stop Loss and Take Profit. Six, Risk-to-Reward.
S03.5 | The last three subtopics are components used in every SOP, but their parameters change with the trading style.
S04.1 | Let's compare the three styles briefly.
S04.2 | Scalping targets short price movements. Context comes from the 1H or 30M, the setup from the 15M, and confirmation from the 5M.
S04.3 | Intraday trading holds positions within the same trading day. Context comes from the Daily and 4H, with structure and setup on the 1H or 30M.
S04.4 | Swing trading holds positions across several sessions or days, with Weekly and Daily context.
S04.5 | The main considerations also differ. Scalping is highly sensitive to costs and execution, intraday to session structure, and swing to HTF context and overnight risk.
S04.6 | This is a CMI teaching framework, not a universal industry standard. Every timeframe combination needs to be tested.
S04.7 | No style is inherently more profitable. What matters is an SOP that is consistent with the chosen style.
S05.1 | Why can't one SOP be used for every style? Look at the stop-loss.
S05.2 | In this hypothetical example, structural invalidation for a scalping setup might be around 10 points, which is 250 ringgit per contract.
S05.3 | For intraday, the relevant structure is larger, for example 18 points, or 450 ringgit. For swing, invalidation on the Daily or 4H might be 80 points, or 2,000 ringgit per contract.
S05.4 | If a scalping stop-loss is used for a swing position, the stop sits inside normal price movement, not at the true invalidation.
S05.5 | Conversely, if a swing stop is used on a scalping setup, the risk becomes far too large compared with the nearby target.
S05.6 | So every style needs its own SOP: timeframes, stop-loss, targets, position size, and how the trade is monitored.
S06.1 | This is the CMI trading workflow, which will be the backbone of Level 9.
S06.2 | It starts with market context and the higher-timeframe bias.
S06.3 | Then choose the trading style. This choice determines the timeframes and the holding period.
S06.4 | Next, map market structure and liquidity, then select the relevant POI.
S06.5 | Entry confirmation follows a sequence: liquidity, MSS or change of character, displacement, then the FVG or execution zone.
S06.6 | Trade planning sets the entry, the structural stop-loss, the liquidity-based take-profit, the risk-to-reward and the position size.
S06.7 | Finally, execution and trade management, followed by the journal and performance evaluation.
S06.8 | Notice that the journal is not an optional extra. Without records, an SOP cannot be evaluated.
S07.1 | Now let's look at a hypothetical FCPO example on the 5-minute chart. This is an educational illustration, not real historical data.
S07.2 | Assume the 1H and 30M context is potentially bullish, and the 15M chart shows a bullish POI around 4,488 to 4,496.
S07.3 | Price is falling. Above, there are two nearly equal highs at 4,520. These are Equal Highs, potential Buy-Side Liquidity.
S07.4 | Below, two lows at 4,494 form Equal Lows, potential Sell-Side Liquidity, sitting inside the POI.
S07.5 | Internal structure is still bearish. The last lower high is at 4,503.
S07.6 | At this point, there is no entry yet. Price is only approaching the selected location. A POI is not an entry.
S08.1 | The next candle drops to 4,491, trading through the Equal Lows, then closes back above 4,494.
S08.2 | This is a possible liquidity sweep. But a sweep on its own is not enough for an entry.
S08.3 | Then a large bullish candle appears, closing at 4,508 and breaking the lower high at 4,503. This is a potential bullish MSS, with displacement.
S08.4 | This displacement leaves a Fair Value Gap between 4,498 and 4,502, the space between the first candle's high and the third candle's low.
S08.5 | Only now is the CMI confirmation sequence complete: liquidity, MSS, displacement and FVG.
S09.1 | Before price moves, we write the trade plan.
S09.2 | A conditional entry at 4,500, inside the FVG, only if price returns to this zone.
S09.3 | Stop-loss at 4,490, one point below the sweep low. If price falls to this level, the bullish hypothesis is considered invalid.
S09.4 | The target is 4,520, the Equal Highs, potential Buy-Side Liquidity on the opposite side.
S09.5 | Risk is 10 points, and potential reward is 20 points. Gross risk-to-reward is 2 to 1.
S09.6 | For one FCPO contract of 25 metric tonnes, each point is worth 25 ringgit. Gross risk is 250 ringgit and gross potential reward is 500 ringgit, before costs and slippage.
S09.7 | Notice that the right side of the chart is still hidden. The decision is made only with the information available.
S10.1 | Now let's reveal what happened next.
S10.2 | Price rises first, then returns to the FVG. The entry order at 4,500 is filled.
S10.3 | After that, price moves higher and reaches 4,520. The target is hit.
S10.4 | But remember, this is just one outcome. With the same setup, price could just as easily have fallen to the stop-loss.
S10.5 | A loss that follows the plan does not mean the SOP was executed wrongly. Strategy performance is judged across a large sample of trades, not a single trade.
S11.1 | One critical point before we continue: margin is not risk.
S11.2 | The notional value of one contract at 4,500 is 4,500 times 25 tonnes, which is 112,500 ringgit.
S11.3 | Even if you use an intraday margin facility, whether half or quarter margin, a 10-point move is still worth 250 ringgit per contract.
S11.4 | Lower margin does not reduce market exposure. It only reduces the capital locked up, and it can encourage oversized positions.
S11.5 | Risk must be calculated from the stop-loss distance, the value per point and the number of contracts, not from the margin.
S12.1 | Before we move to the first SOP, drop these five misconceptions.
S12.2 | One: every liquidity sweep produces a reversal. No. A sweep is only a possibility.
S12.3 | Two: price touching a POI means entry. No. A POI is a location, not a trigger.
S12.4 | Three: a 1 to 3 risk-to-reward guarantees profit. No. The outcome depends on win rate and costs.
S12.5 | Four: lower margin is safer. No. Price exposure stays the same.
S12.6 | Five: one SOP fits every style. No. Each style needs its own SOP.
S12.7 | Remember, SMC concepts are analytical frameworks. Chart patterns do not prove institutional identity, position size or intent.
S13.1 | Knowledge check. Try to answer before the answer appears.
S13.2 | Question one: what is the difference between a setup and a trading plan?
S13.3 | Answer: a setup is a hypothesis at a location. A trading plan sets the rules for entry, stop-loss, target, risk and no-trade conditions.
S13.4 | Question two: entry 4,500, stop-loss 4,490, target 4,520. What is the gross risk-to-reward?
S13.5 | Answer: 20 points of reward divided by 10 points of risk, which is 2 to 1.
S13.6 | Question three: if you use quarter margin, does a 10-point risk become smaller?
S13.7 | Answer: no. It stays at 250 ringgit per contract, before costs.
