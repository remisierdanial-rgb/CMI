S01.5 | Finally, we map market structure, liquidity and POIs specifically for scalping.
S02.1 | What is scalping? Scalping is a short-term trading style with a brief holding period, usually measured in minutes.
S02.2 | The first characteristic: a short holding period. Stop-losses and targets are also closer than in other trading styles.
S02.3 | Second, opportunities appear frequently. But a good SOP stays selective, and only takes setups that meet its conditions.
S02.4 | Third, speed of analysis and execution matters, because the moves being targeted are small.
S02.5 | Fourth, transaction costs have a large impact. When the target is small, costs and slippage take a bigger share of the reward.
S02.6 | Fifth, risk management must be strict, with a risk limit per trade and a daily loss limit.
S02.7 | Scalping is not simply getting in and out quickly. It needs a setup, an entry condition, an invalidation and an exit plan defined in advance.
S03.1 | Do more trades mean more profit? Not necessarily.
S03.2 | Assume, hypothetically, that costs and slippage are equivalent to two points per trade.
S03.3 | A trader taking 20 trades a day pays costs equivalent to 40 points, before making any profit.
S03.4 | A trader who only takes three quality setups pays costs equivalent to six points.
S03.5 | For a 10-point target, a two-point cost is 20 percent of the reward. For a 40-point target, it is only 5 percent.
S03.6 | That is why scalping is so sensitive to costs. Frequency does not replace a strategy's edge.
S04.1 | Next, the CMI scalping timeframe framework. This is an illustrative framework for FCPO, not an optimal formula.
S04.2 | The 1H or 30M chart provides context: direction, major structure and key levels.
S04.3 | The 15M chart is used to identify the setup and the location: liquidity and POIs.
S04.4 | The 5M chart is used to evaluate entry confirmation: MSS, displacement and FVG.
S04.5 | The 1M chart is optional. It can refine the entry, but it contains more noise and false signals, and needs suitable data and testing.
S04.6 | The right timeframe combination must be tested and adapted. Don't assume it is optimal for everyone.
S05.1 | See how these three timeframes connect.
S05.2 | On the 1H, structure makes higher highs and higher lows. The context is potentially bullish.
S05.3 | On the 15M, price pulls back into a POI in discount, near Sell-Side Liquidity.
S05.4 | On the 5M, we wait for a sweep, an MSS and displacement before considering an entry.
S05.5 | Each timeframe answers a different question: where is direction, where is the location, and when is the trigger.
S06.1 | Now, pre-market preparation. These ten steps are done before the session starts, not while price is moving.
S06.2 | One, check the FCPO trading calendar and the current session. Two, review how the previous session moved.
S06.3 | Three and four, mark the Previous Day High and the Previous Day Low.
S06.4 | Five, identify significant Weekly and Daily levels. Six, mark the relevant liquidity pools.
S06.5 | Seven, set a preliminary bias: bullish, bearish or neutral. Eight, identify POIs on the higher and medium timeframes.
S06.6 | Nine, check market information and scheduled events, for example the monthly MPOB report.
S06.7 | Ten, define the maximum acceptable risk for today, in ringgit, before opening any position.
S07.1 | This is what a prepared chart looks like.
S07.2 | PDH and PDL are marked as the boundaries of yesterday's range, and also as potential liquidity.
S07.3 | Equal Lows below and the session high above are marked as liquidity pools.
S07.4 | One POI is selected. The preliminary bias is written down together with the condition that would invalidate it.
S07.5 | A good chart is not crowded. Mark only the levels you will actually use in your decisions.
S08.1 | Next, market structure for scalping. These terms are the same as in the previous levels.
S08.2 | Higher highs and higher lows show bullish structure. Lower highs and lower lows show bearish structure.
S08.3 | A BOS is a close beyond a swing in the direction of the trend. A CHoCH is the first break against the trend.
S08.4 | An MSS is a structure shift accompanied by displacement, usually after a liquidity interaction.
S08.5 | External structure defines the main range. Internal structure is the smaller swings inside it.
S08.6 | Displacement is a strong directional move, with large candle bodies and range expansion. In scalping, it helps separate a short-term change of direction from noise.
S09.1 | A common scalping mistake is treating every small break as a change of direction.
S09.2 | On the left, the 1H is still bullish. A small internal break on the 5M is just a pullback within the trend.
S09.3 | On the right, price sweeps an external low, then breaks structure with displacement. This is a more meaningful shift.
S09.4 | Before acting, ask: is this break internal and minor, or does it change the bigger context?
S10.1 | Next, liquidity mapping for scalping.
S10.2 | Buy-Side Liquidity sits above highs. Sell-Side Liquidity sits below lows.
S10.3 | Equal Highs and Equal Lows are nearly identical highs or lows that form potential liquidity pools.
S10.4 | The session high, session low, PDH and PDL are daily references that scalpers use often.
S10.5 | Internal liquidity sits inside the range. External liquidity sits beyond the major swings.
S10.6 | A liquidity sweep happens when price trades through a level and then closes back inside the range.
S11.1 | Distinguish a sweep from a breakout.
S11.2 | A sweep: price trades through the Equal Lows, but closes back above them. This is a possible liquidity grab.
S11.3 | A breakout: price closes and is accepted below the level. Bearish structure continues.
S11.4 | Not every liquidity level gets taken, and not every sweep leads to a reversal. A sweep is only one piece of evidence.
S12.1 | Next, POI selection using the CMI operational definitions.
S12.2 | An Order Block is the last opposing candle before a displacement that breaks structure.
S12.3 | A Fair Value Gap is a three-candle imbalance. A Breaker Block is a failed Order Block that then works from the opposite side after a sweep.
S12.4 | A Mitigation Block is similar, but without a sweep of the prior high or low. A Rejection Block is a long-wick area at a swing extreme.
S12.5 | Supply and Demand zones are the origin zones of strong moves.
S12.6 | For scalping, select at most two or three POIs that align with the context. Too many zones make every price look like an opportunity.
S13.1 | Remember, price touching a POI is not confirmation.
S13.2 | In this example, price enters a bullish POI, then breaks straight through it without any reaction.
S13.3 | A trader who buys just because price touched the zone gets stopped out.
S13.4 | That is why we need entry confirmation, which we cover in Video 3.
S14.1 | Four common preparation mistakes.
S14.2 | One: no daily risk limit before the session starts.
S14.3 | Two: so many lines and zones that the chart can't be read.
S14.4 | Three: changing the bias on every small candle.
S14.5 | Four: ignoring the schedule of events that can cause unusual volatility.
S15.2 | Question one: what is the main difference between scalping, intraday and swing trading?
S15.3 | Answer: the holding period and the timeframe framework. These lead to different stop-losses, targets, position sizes and overnight exposure.
S15.4 | Question two: why is higher-timeframe context important before scalping?
S15.5 | Answer: it provides direction and location, so a minor break on the 5M is not mistaken for a trend change.
S15.6 | Question three: is the 1M chart required for scalping?
S15.7 | Answer: no. It is optional, noisier, and only used with suitable data and testing.
S16.2 | Scalping means short holding periods, selectivity, sensitivity to costs and strict risk limits.
S16.3 | Context on the 1H or 30M, location on the 15M, the trigger on the 5M, and the 1M as an option.
S16.4 | Ten pre-market preparation steps, including a daily risk limit in ringgit.
S16.5 | Map structure, liquidity, and two or three POIs. Touching a POI is not confirmation.
S17.2 | Prepare one FCPO 15M chart. Mark PDH, PDL, one Weekly or Daily level, Equal Highs or Equal Lows, and the session high and low.
S17.3 | Then write a prep sheet: bias and invalidation, two or three POIs, scheduled events, today's maximum risk and the maximum number of trades.
