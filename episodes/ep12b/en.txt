S14.5 | Was the strategy executed wrongly? No. Every condition was met, the size was correct, and the stop was not moved. This is a normal outcome of a strategy, not an execution error.
S15.1 | Distinguish a failed setup from poor execution.
S15.2 | A failed setup: every rule was followed, but the market did not move as expected. This is a normal part of any strategy.
S15.3 | Poor execution: a rule was broken. An early entry, a moved stop, the wrong size, or a trade without confirmation.
S15.4 | The journal lets you tell the two apart. Without a journal, every loss looks the same.
S16.1 | Case eight: a no-trade scenario.
S16.2 | The context conflicts: the 1H is bullish, but the Daily is bearish and price is in premium.
S16.3 | Price has not interacted with any meaningful liquidity, and the entry location is in the middle of the range.
S16.4 | There is no structure shift with displacement. And the nearest target is only a few points away, so the RR is insufficient.
S16.5 | The decision: no trade. Not entering is also a valid trading decision, and it is recorded in the journal.
S17.2 | Question one: why must losing trades be recorded?
S17.3 | Answer: so the metrics are not biased, and execution errors become visible.
S17.4 | Question two: what is the difference between a failed setup and poor execution?
S17.5 | Answer: a failed setup means the rules were followed but the market disagreed. Poor execution means a rule was broken.
S17.6 | Question three: is not trading a trading decision?
S17.7 | Answer: yes, and it is recorded in the journal like any other decision.
